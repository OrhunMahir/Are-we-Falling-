TOWER EYES ONLY

ARE WE FALLING?

Tower Emergency Handbook · Munich Approach

An aircraft is falling out of the sky and you are the only one with the handbook. The pilot can see the cockpit but has no idea what to do. You know what to do but cannot see the cockpit.

Type the four digits the pilot reads to you. In an online lobby the code arrives by itself.
  • Never show this handbook to the pilot, and never look at the pilot's screen. Use your voice.
  • The cockpit has about eighty panels and hundreds of switches. No panel has a name. Each one only carries a small picture in its top left corner, and the pictures are dealt again on every flight.
  • Only a few systems are failing. The pilot sees a list called FAILED SYSTEMS and a four-digit FLIGHT CODE in the top right corner. Ask for the code and type it in above: the handbook then shows which picture marks each failed panel on this flight.
  • You tell the pilot which picture to look for ("find the bell panel"). A small red light blinks in the top right corner of a failing panel. Panels sit in a different place on every flight: front, ceiling, floor console, side walls, even the wall behind the seat. Tell the pilot to look around and zoom in.
  • Panels on the walls behind the seats are out of reach from the seat. The pilot has to stand up (F key) to use them, and sit down again (F) to use anything at the front.
  • All the other panels have pictures too. A wrong switch means turbulence: the aircraft shakes and the passengers get angrier, so do not let the pilot try things at random. Knobs: the pilot clicks a knob, the view moves in, then turns it with the scroll wheel, by dragging sideways or with A and D, and clicks again to let go.
  • There is a bottle of whiskey on top of the instrument panel. Every sip buys the pilot 8 seconds, but the cockpit gets blurry and the labels hard to read for a while. Six sips and the bottle is empty. Decide together when it is worth it.
  • A wrong action costs altitude. Three wrong actions and the aircraft is lost.
  • Every flight is different. Do not guess from the last one.

The placard. One panel is the aircraft data placard. It sits in the middle of the front instrument panel, straight ahead of the pilot, and its picture is in the flight code table once you have the code. Several chapters need its facts, so ask for them first:

FLIGHTe.g. AWF 519. You usually need the last digit.
REGISTRATIONe.g. D-AASK. You usually need the last three letters.
SOULS ON BOARDPeople in the aircraft.
APU and ICE lightsEach one is either lit or dark.
