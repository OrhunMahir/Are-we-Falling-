### The contract between the game and the tower room

The tower room does not know the game. It only receives a small state object, so both sides can change freely as long as this stays the same.

`window.KKTower.start(canvas, opts)` returns `{ setState(st), state, setPeek(bool), stop() }`. `opts` is optional: `{ view: 'outside' }` films the same scene from outside cameras (chase, runway side, a shot of the tower) instead of the controller's seat, for end screens or the cabin seats later.

The game (pilot side, `js/net.js`) sends `st` twice a second; the tower view passes it into the handbook frame with `postMessage({ awf: 1, st })`:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `f` | flight code (four digits) |
| `ph` | phase: `lobby`, `intro`, `flight`, `landing`, `won`, `crashed` |
| `tl`, `tot` | seconds left, seconds in total |
| `k` | wrong actions so far (0 to 3) |
| `s`, `n` | failures solved, failures in total |
| `o` | ids of the failures still open: `breakers`, `fire`, `radio`, `hyd`, `intercom`, `gear`, `xpdr`, `keys` |

Today the tower room uses it like this: the aircraft comes closer as time runs out; flame and dark smoke while `fire` is open or `k` is 2 or more; white mist while `hyd` is open; flickering lights while `breakers` is open. On `crashed` it banks round and flies into the tower windows (4 to 7 s), then the impact plays in the cab. On `landing` and `won` it flies a curve down to the runway and touches down **6.5 s after the phase starts** (so it lines up with the touchdown sound the pilot screen plays at 7 s), then brakes to a stop. Without a link it plays a demo loop.

If a new feature needs a new field, Orhun adds it to `st` and writes it into this table.

Two more places use the tower room, so please keep them working when you change it:

- **The landing shot on the pilot's screen.** When the flight is saved, `js/game.js` (`startLandCam`) starts a second copy of the tower room on the pilot's screen with `KKTower.start(canvas)`, calls `setPeek(true)`, sets `state.p` and `state.mx / state.my` directly and sends `ph: 'flight'` followed by `ph: 'landing'`. It is shown zoomed in on the runway (`#landCam` in `css/cockpit3d.css`). So `start`, `setState`, `setPeek`, `stop` and the `state` fields `p`, `mx`, `my` must stay.
- **The tower looks up by itself.** On `landing`, `won` and `crashed` the tower room turns the head to the aircraft on its own (`tower3d.js`), and `manual.html` wraps `setState` so the handbook is put down (`body.endview`) whatever sends the state; it comes back with the next flight. This replaces the earlier `onPhase` helper.

## The four seats

A flight needs only two seats, **pilot** and **tower**. The other two are optional, anyone who wants one takes it, and the game adapts to whoever is missing.

| Seat | Needed | Where | Goal | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | yes | cockpit | fix the failures before the time runs out | Orhun |
| Tower | yes | tower room | read the handbook to the pilot | Orhun (handbook), Efe (tower room) |
| Intruder | optional | cabin, among the passengers | make the aircraft go down | **Batu** |
| Flight attendant | optional | cabin | keep the cabin calm and the saboteur busy | **Efe** |

The earlier idea of a second villain seat (a helper in a basement with a manual of their own) is **cancelled**. The fourth seat is the flight attendant.

**Who wins.** Pilot, tower and flight attendant are one team. They win when the aircraft lands. The saboteur wins when it does not. Everything the two cabin seats do moves the same clock the pilot is racing: the time to impact.

### Saboteur (Batu)

- Sits in the cabin as one of the passengers.
- Every flight deals a few **sabotage tasks from a pool, at random**, so no two flights are the same. Examples from Orhun: go into the lavatory and block the ventilation, clog the toilet, stir up the other passengers. Batu designs the pool: what each task is, where it happens, what the player has to do on screen, how long it takes.
- Can **stand up and sit down in any empty seat**, to get near a task or away from the flight attendant.
- **Every finished sabotage shortens the time to impact.**
- If nobody takes the seat there is no saboteur and the flight plays as it does today.

### Flight attendant (Efe)

- Works in the cabin.
- **Calms the passengers.** With a flight attendant on board this is their job; without one the pilot keeps doing it over the PA, as today.
- **Stalls and distracts the saboteur**, so that sabotage takes longer or fails.
- Has a **task pool of their own**, dealt at random like the saboteur's. Efe designs it.
- **Every finished task gives the pilot extra time.**
- Stretch goal, only if there is time at the very end: the flight attendant can catch the saboteur.

### The cabin (first version is in)

The cabin is a room of its own in `js/cabin3d.js`, and `cabin.html` is a test page for it. It is not linked from the game yet, so it cannot break the submission. **Batu and Efe: this is where you start.**

Open it with a static server (`python3 -m http.server 8765`, then `http://localhost:8765/cabin.html`):

- `cabin.html?role=saboteur` starts in a free seat among the passengers.
- `cabin.html?role=attendant` starts standing in the galley.
- `&seed=1234` gives the same passengers and the same deal of tasks every time.

Controls: drag to look, click a free seat or a door to walk there, W and S walk along the aisle, F stands up or sits down in the nearest free seat, hold E to work on a task when you are in the right place. The box in the lower left corner makes the passengers angry, shakes the cabin and opens the cockpit door, for testing.

The page reads `KK.ROLES[role]`, deals `perFlight` tasks, marks their places with a ring on the floor, runs the hold timer and adds `effect` to a stand-in clock. So a task pool can be written and tried today, without waiting for the lobby.

What is in the room: nine rows, seats A to F, about two thirds taken; the galley and the cockpit door at the front; the lavatory (toilet, sink, ventilation grille) at the rear left; a second galley at the rear right.

The cabin does not know the game or the tasks. It offers this, and the game side uses it:

| Call | What it does |
|---|---|
| `KKCabin.start(canvas, { seed, role, seat })` | builds the cabin and returns the object below. `seed` is the flight code, so every screen gets the same passengers |
| `goTo(target)` | walks to `'door'`, `'galley'`, `'aisle'`, `'lavatory'` or to a seat id such as `'4C'` (and sits down) |
| `at()` | `{ place, seat, x, z }`, `place` is `seat`, `aisle`, `lavatory`, `galley`, `door` or `moving` |
| `on('arrive' / 'leave' / 'hover', fn)` | events |
| `seats`, `places` | every seat with `id`, `row`, `taken`; the four places with their position |
| `setState({ unrest, turb })` | 0 to 1 each: angry passengers, shaking |
| `setMarks(['lavatory'])` | rings on the floor where something is to do |
| `setActor(id, { role, at })` | shows another player at a place or in a seat, `null` removes them |
| `setDoor(true)` | opens or closes the cockpit door |
| `stop()` | ends it |

Not there yet: the cabin is not in the lobby, the clock on the page is not the real one, the other player on the page is a dummy that does not move, and nobody can interrupt anybody. If a task needs something the cabin does not have (an object to click, a new place, a way to stall the saboteur), tell Orhun.

### How the three parts fit together

So that three people can work at once without touching the same files, each cabin seat is one file that only describes its tasks, and the game does the rest.

`js/roles/saboteur.js` and `js/roles/attendant.js` each register a pool. Both files exist as starters with three example tasks each; Batu and Efe replace the examples with their own:

```js
KK.ROLES = KK.ROLES || {};
KK.ROLES.saboteur = {
  perFlight: 3,                      // how many tasks one flight deals from the pool
  tasks: [
    {
      id: 'vent',                    // unique in the pool
      title: 'BLOCK THE VENT',       // shown to the player
      place: 'lavatory',             // where in the cabin: 'seat' (sitting in any seat), 'aisle', 'lavatory', 'galley', 'door'
      brief: 'Go into the lavatory and stuff the ventilation grille.',
      seconds: 14,                   // how long the player is busy with it
      effect: -20,                   // seconds added to the time to impact: negative for the saboteur, positive for the attendant
    },
  ],
};
```

The game side, written by Orhun, deals the tasks from the flight code (so every screen agrees), moves the players through the cabin, runs the timer of a task, applies `effect` to the time to impact, tells the pilot and the tower what happened, and syncs it over the lobby. If a task needs more than "go there and hold for a few seconds" (a small puzzle, a choice, something the attendant can interrupt), describe it in `brief` and talk to Orhun: the interface grows with what the tasks need.

Places, numbers and the field names above are a first proposal, not final.
